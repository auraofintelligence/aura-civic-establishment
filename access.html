KYC and a shared place

Know who is entering. Make the purpose clear.

The operating direction is 24/7 shared use with KYC, meaning identity verification appropriate to the facility relationship.

A membership account can establish who the person is. A permission identifies the spaces, equipment or activities available to them. A booking allocates time. The activity host carries the relevant service responsibilities.

Personal Aura records keep their own chosen audiences and permissions. Building access, a teaching portfolio and a private reflection each have a distinct purpose.

A policy to develop with people

Identity → permission → booking → handover

Agree what information the facility needs, who performs verification, who can review it, how long it is retained and how a person corrects an error or changes their access.

The examples below explore a future facility policy. This public planning site does not collect identity documents or issue building credentials.

The right relationship for the room.

A hosted introduction

A guest can explore an introductory activity through the agreed welcome and host arrangements. Access to other spaces follows the selected activity and facility policy.

Illustrative relationships for discussion. The future operator determines its actual access policy, verification process and service arrangements.

A few relationships to make understandable

Access should be easy to explain.

RelationshipPossible arrangement to develop
GuestAn understandable invitation, an identified host and a way to obtain assistance.
Verified memberAgreed identity checks, facility terms, an account recovery route and bookable access.
Activity hostRelevant room permissions, participant arrangements, setup and handover responsibilities.
Technical operatorEquipment-specific permissions, maintenance windows and an operating record.
Service providerA defined service, its staffing and records, and the rooms needed for appointments or activities.

Dignity and continuity

People need a way through an error.

Verification can fail, a pass can be lost and a booking can change. The operating brief includes an assisted route, a responsible human contact, a review process and what happens outside ordinary reception hours.

A person should understand which part of the relationship is changing. A late booking, a service appointment and a technical permission have different consequences. Privacy-sensitive activity needs rooms and records appropriate to that activity.

A useful design tests the whole experience with participants: arrival, finding the room, understanding their activity, getting help and leaving the space ready for the next booking.
